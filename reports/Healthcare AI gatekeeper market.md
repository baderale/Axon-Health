# Narrow the gatekeeper, then prove buyers exist

Axon Health's gatekeeper is worth more building, but only as a narrow product with a 90-day test, not as the full "federated subsidiaries" platform. The pain is real and documented. In 2026, hospital staff pasted patient data into personal ChatGPT accounts and research teams uploaded medical record numbers to unapproved AI tools. Surveys say more than half of healthcare workers have met or used unapproved AI, and only about a quarter of organizations enforce a written AI policy. The money is real too. Big security companies paid $150M to $700M for AI-firewall startups in 2025, and seed rounds for AI-agent security run $3M to $8.6M this fall. But three facts make the case weaker than it looks. OpenAI and Anthropic now sign HIPAA contracts (BAAs) directly, which removes the old "we legally can't send patient data" reason to buy. Federal rules are loosening, not tightening. And Axon's 83% recall (it catches about 5 of every 6 identifiers) sits well below the 94% to 98% that commercial tools claim. A realistic base case is about $8M in yearly recurring revenue by year 5, if a seed round closes in 2027. As things stand (solo founder, no customers), the odds of raising an institutional seed within 12 months are low, about 3% to 6% by my estimate. Two or three signed design partners roughly triple those odds, and a co-founder plus partners roughly quintuple them. The next 90 days should go to finding buyers, not writing code.

**Terms used in this report.** *PHI* (protected health information) is health data tied to a person. The *18 Safe Harbor identifiers* are the items HIPAA says to remove (names, dates, record numbers, and so on). A *BAA* (business associate agreement) is the contract that lets a vendor legally handle PHI. *Recall* is the share of real identifiers a tool catches. *ARR* is annual recurring revenue. *ACV* is the average yearly contract value. *TAM/SAM/SOM* stand for the total market, the part you can serve, and the part you can realistically win. A *design partner* is an early customer who tests the product and gives feedback, often unpaid or at a discount. An *LOI* (letter of intent) is a written, non-binding promise to buy. A *SAFE* is a simple early investment contract that turns into shares later.

## Staff are already leaking PHI into AI, and that is the clearest use today

The best-documented need is not agent-to-agent traffic. It is people. At a Washington state psychiatric facility, a staff member "used their personal ChatGPT account to draft summaries for client case notes," including names, diagnoses and medications, several times before anyone noticed ([HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/healthcare-providers-unauthorized-disclosure-phi-ai-tools/)). At Weill Cornell, radiology data from NewYork-Presbyterian was entered into unauthorized AI tools in June 2026. It held **medical record numbers, accession numbers, service dates and diagnoses, but no names or Social Security numbers** ([HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/healthcare-providers-unauthorized-disclosure-phi-ai-tools/)). That second case matters for Axon. A tool that only strips names would have missed it. Record numbers and dates are Safe Harbor identifiers too, and Axon's pattern rules for "shaped" identifiers are built for exactly those.

Surveys show the problem is wide. In a Wolters Kluwer survey of 518 providers and administrators, **57% had met or used unapproved AI tools**. One in three said the reason was that no approved tool did the job ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260122063603/en)). Black Book found 58% had used generic AI tools for work in the last 30 days, and **only 24% of organizations had "a clear, documented, enforced staff policy"** ([DH Insights](https://dhinsights.org/news/shadow-ai-emerges-as-one-of-healthcares-biggest-security-risks)). The root cause, "no approved tool," points to a product insight. Hospitals do not need another blocker. They need a safe, approved path. Axon's "coach" verdict, which explains a problem and sends the message back instead of silently killing it, fits that need better than a hard block.

Three uses are realistic today. The first is a **self-hosted proxy for digital health startups and AI vendors** that call model APIs. It scrubs PHI before data leaves, routes to local models or vendors that lack a BAA, and keeps one audit log across all of them. Builders clearly feel this pain: open-source projects like phi-redactor exist for this exact job ([GitHub](https://github.com/DilawarShafiq/phi-redactor)), and so do paid tools like Redact-Proxy ([redact.health](https://www.redact.health/redact-proxy)). The second is a **sanctioned AI gateway for a health system's staff**, so the approved tool is easier than the personal ChatGPT account. The third is **evidence for an AI governance committee**. The Joint Commission and the Coalition for Health AI (CHAI) published voluntary guidance in September 2025 that asks for ongoing monitoring and data security for AI ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/chai-and-joint-commission-release-guiding-document-health-systems-using-ai)), and a voluntary certification followed ([Joint Commission guide](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/7bd26620a3e54c0ead2138afa37fc167?v=5f349b78)). A per-message audit trail is the kind of proof that helps.

One use is **not** realistic yet: de-identifying data for research release. With 83% recall, about one identifier in six gets through. Today the product must be sold as a backstop that sits behind a BAA, not as a guarantee. Axon's most distinctive feature, gating messages between AI agents, is also ahead of demand. The research found **no buyer or practitioner asking for an agent-to-agent PHI gatekeeper**, and no funding round for one. Keep it in the design, but do not lead the pitch with it.

### What practitioners say, and what is missing

Developer opinion on Hacker News is mostly "sign a BAA and you're on your own." One commenter put it simply: "You need to sign a BAA" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50416163)). Others fear leaks: "The chance of data leaking out through the LLM or hacks is too high" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41482710)). Skeptics call redaction a "band-aid" and prefer hardware-sealed computing ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43996555)). Vendors repeat the main objection a buyer will raise: "one false negative means patient data left your boundary" ([Respan](https://www.respan.ai/resources/hipaa-baa-llm-engineering)). **Reddit could not be searched.** The research tools were blocked from it, so clinician voices from r/medicine or r/healthIT are missing. That gap should be filled by direct interviews, which are more useful anyway.

## BAAs and deregulation weaken the "must buy" reason

The biggest threat to the business case is not a competitor. It is that the legal reason to redact is fading. **OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Healthcare with BAAs in January 2026**, with rollouts at HCA, AdventHealth, Cedars-Sinai and others ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/08/openai-chatgpt-doctors-patients-health-tab)). Anthropic followed days later with Claude for Healthcare, also covered by BAAs and available through the major clouds ([Paubox](https://www.paubox.com/blog/anthropic-brings-claude-ai-to-healthcare-with-hipaa-tools)). When the model vendor signs a BAA, a hospital may legally send PHI. Redaction becomes "nice to have."

Federal rules are moving the same way. The proposed HIPAA Security Rule overhaul from January 2025 is still not final, and its target has slipped to **July 2027** ([Pact-One](https://www.pact-one.com/2026/09/hipaa-security-rule-2026-status/)). ONC's HTI-5 rule, released around October 26, 2026, **removed** earlier transparency and risk-management requirements for predictive AI tools ([Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/onc-issues-hti-5-final-rule-removing-model-card-and-risk-management-requirements-for-predictive-decision-support-interventions)). State laws in California and Texas focus on telling patients when AI is used and on human review, not on data flow ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=801f0844-1f2b-4423-a98d-1e0f3f3b61ca)). No US rule today requires an AI gatekeeper.

Several reasons to buy survive the BAA. A BAA does not cover the customer's own duties. "You remain responsible for de-identification, audit logging, encryption, access control, redaction" ([Respan](https://www.respan.ai/resources/hipaa-baa-llm-engineering)). It does not stop staff using personal accounts, as the Washington case showed. It does not cover local or multi-vendor models. And it does not stop AI agents from being tricked. A June 2026 preprint found a general prompt-injection detector caught only **40% of healthcare-specific attacks, versus 83% for a firewall that checks scope and PHI** ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.04.26354950v2.full)). These reasons are real, but they are softer. Axon should sell **risk reduction and audit evidence**, not legal necessity. Breach costs make the risk concrete: the average healthcare breach cost more than **$7.4M in 2025** ([AHCJ](https://healthjournalism.org/blog/2026/02/shadow-ai-on-the-rise-in-health-care-as-patients-report-less-trust-surveys-say/)).

## A crowded field with one open lane

The market splits into six groups. None of them combines all of Axon's parts.

| Group | Examples | What they do well | What they miss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cheap cloud redaction APIs | Google DLP (~$0.03 per 1M characters), AWS Comprehend (~$1-2 per 1M), Azure (~$1.67-6.67 per 1M), free Presidio | Price; scale | No HIPAA reasoning; weaker on clinical text |
| Premium clinical de-identification | John Snow Labs, Limina (formerly Private AI) | Claimed 0.96-0.98 accuracy; self-hosting | Built for documents and datasets, not live agent traffic |
| Healthcare LLM gateways | Redact-Proxy, nRouter, Concentrate, Tetrate, Scrub AI, Mintive | Drop-in PHI redaction in front of model APIs | Redact only; no judges; no published recall |
| General AI security (mostly acquired) | Protect AI (Palo Alto), Lakera (Check Point), CalypsoAI (F5), Prompt Security (SentinelOne) | Prompt-injection defense; sold through big security platforms | Not HIPAA-specific |
| Health AI governance platforms | Qualified Health, Credo AI, Censinet | Policy, inventory, risk reviews | Not in the message path |
| Model vendors and clouds | ChatGPT for Healthcare, Claude for Healthcare, Bedrock Guardrails | Bundled with the BAA | Single-vendor; generic guardrails |

Sources for the table: cloud pricing ([Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/pii-redaction-pricing-comprehend-azure-google-presidio-cost-per-million-characters)); John Snow Labs claims ([JSL benchmarks 2026](https://www.johnsnowlabs.com/clinical-de-identification-benchmarks-2026-john-snow-labs-against-openai-databricks-presidio-and-llm-apis/)); gateways ([nRouter](https://nrouter.ai/solutions/healthcare), [Concentrate](https://concentrate.ai/blog/how-to-use-gpt-and-claude-with-phi-through-a-hipaa-compliant-ai-gateway-and-a-baa), [Tetrate](https://tetrate.io/learn/ai/hipaa-ai-gateway-healthcare), [Scrub](https://scrub.health/)); acquisitions ([Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news-features/cybersecurity-ma-crowdstrike/), [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/securing-ais-ma-feeding-frenzy-is-on/)); governance ([Fierce Healthcare](https://fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/qualified-health-locks-125m-fresh-funding-scale-ai-health-systems)).

Two things stand out. First, **pure redaction is a commodity**. Google charges about 3 cents per million characters, so Axon cannot win on redaction price. It must charge for the judges, the audit trail and the deployment, not per character ([Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/pii-redaction-pricing-comprehend-azure-google-presidio-cost-per-million-characters)). Second, there is a **gap between the governance platforms and the gateways**. Governance tools write policy but do not sit in the message path. Gateways sit in the path but only redact. Axon does both: it redacts with fixed rules, lets AI judges decide whether a message may go, and logs every step. It runs fully on the customer's own servers, judges included, so no PHI leaves the network. And it measures both recall and "clinical text damage" (over-redaction of doses and drug names), which **no vendor found in this research publishes**. That is the open lane.

### Where 83% recall really stands

Accuracy is the first thing a buyer will ask about. Here is how 83% compares, with a big caveat: every number below uses a different test set, so the comparisons only show direction.

| Detector | Recall or F1 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| John Snow Labs (vendor claim) | 0.98 recall, 0.96 F1 | [JSL 2026](https://www.johnsnowlabs.com/clinical-de-identification-benchmarks-2026-john-snow-labs-against-openai-databricks-presidio-and-llm-apis/) |
| AWS Comprehend Medical | 0.94-0.96 F1 (100 i2b2 notes) | [arXiv 2312.08495](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.08495) |
| Best 2014 i2b2 research system | 0.936 F1 | [Stubbs et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989908) |
| Frontier LLMs (Claude, GPT, Gemini) | 0.86-0.91 F1 | [JSL 2026](https://www.johnsnowlabs.com/clinical-de-identification-benchmarks-2026-john-snow-labs-against-openai-databricks-presidio-and-llm-apis/) |
| **Axon Health (blind holdout)** | **0.83 recall** | Internal eval |
| Single human annotator (average) | 0.81 recall | [JSL 2026, citing Neamatullah 2008](https://www.johnsnowlabs.com/clinical-de-identification-benchmarks-2026-john-snow-labs-against-openai-databricks-presidio-and-llm-apis/) |
| Presidio out of the box (clinical notes) | 0.74-0.81 recall | [JSL 2026](https://www.johnsnowlabs.com/clinical-de-identification-benchmarks-2026-john-snow-labs-against-openai-databricks-presidio-and-llm-apis/) |
| OpenAI Privacy Filter (JSL-run test) | 0.64 recall | [JSL 2026](https://www.johnsnowlabs.com/clinical-de-identification-benchmarks-2026-john-snow-labs-against-openai-databricks-presidio-and-llm-apis/) |

Axon beats free open-source tools and a single human reader, but trails commercial tools by 10 to 15 points. The honest framing is a strength: Axon's holdout was written blind and scored once, while most vendor numbers are self-run. To compete, the target should be **95%+ recall on a fresh blind holdout**, ideally plus a run on the standard i2b2 2014 test set. Weak spots across the whole field are contact details, ID numbers and addresses ([JSL 2026](https://www.johnsnowlabs.com/clinical-de-identification-benchmarks-2026-john-snow-labs-against-openai-databricks-presidio-and-llm-apis/)). Those are "shaped" identifiers, where Axon's pattern rules should win.

## Revenue: about $185M serviceable US market, $8M ARR by year 5 in the base case

**TAM (total market).** The closest analyst category is Gartner's "securing AI" market: about **$2.8B in 2026, rising to $4.8B in 2027 and $7.7B by 2028**, worldwide and across all industries ([IT Brief, citing Gartner](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/gartner-sees-securing-ai-market-hit-usd-4-8bn-in-2027)). Inside that, AI governance platforms are small, about $275M to $492M in 2026 (two Gartner figures conflict) ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/gartner-sees-securing-ai-market-hit-usd-4-8bn-in-2027); [Creati.ai](https://creati.ai/ai-news/2026-02-17/gartner-ai-governance-platforms-market-1-billion-2030)). No credible healthcare-only number exists. If healthcare is 8% to 12% of that spend, which is my assumption, healthcare "securing AI" is about **$0.25B to $0.6B by 2027**. Ignore the "$25B to $40B healthcare AI market" headlines. Menlo Ventures measured actual US healthcare generative-AI spending at only **about $1.4B in 2025**, three times 2024 ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/menlo_ventures_healthcare_ai_report-2025.pdf)).

**SAM (the part Axon can serve).** Counted from the bottom up, the US has **397 health systems** ([AHA](https://www.aha.org/infographics/2025-06-18-fast-facts-us-health-systems-2025-infographic)) and about 240 AI-enabled digital health companies funded in 2025 alone ([Rock Health](https://rockhealth.com/rock-weekly/2025-year-end-funding-14-2b-raised-across-482-deals/)). Using assumed prices (none are published for this category): about 400 health systems at $200K, plus about 300 payers and pharma companies at $200K, plus about 1,500 AI-using startups and vendors at $30K, gives roughly **$80M + $60M + $45M ≈ $185M a year in the US**. The payer, pharma and startup counts are estimates and should be checked.

**SOM (what Axon can win).** In the base case, about **$8.5M ARR by year 5, or roughly 4% to 5% of SAM**. That equals about 25 enterprise customers, around 6% of US health systems or their payer and pharma equivalents.

The ramp below assumes sales go to startups and AI vendors first, because they buy in weeks, and to health systems second, because they take 6 to 18 months ([SyncGTM](https://syncgtm.com/blog/b2b-medical-sales)). Year 1 is 2027. All ACVs are assumptions to test in discovery calls.

| Year-end ARR | Downside (solo, no seed) | Base (seed closes mid-2027) | Upside (accelerator + co-founder) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 (2027) | $0.05M: 2-3 small startups | **$0.15M**: 5 startups at $20K + 1 health-system pilot at $50K | $0.4M: 10 at $25K + 2 pilots at $75K |
| Year 2 (2028) | $0.2M | **$0.85M**: 18 at $30K + 2 enterprise at $150K | $1.8M: 30 at $35K + 4 at $190K |
| Year 3 (2029) | $0.5M | **$2.4M**: 35 at $35K + 6 at $200K | $4.5M: 50 at $40K + 12 at $210K |
| Year 4 (2030) | $0.9M | **$5.0M**: 55 at $40K + 14 at $200K | $9.0M: 80 at $45K + 25 at $215K |
| Year 5 (2031) | $1.5M | **$8.5M**: 75 at $40K + 25 at $220K | $15M: 110 at $45K + 45 at $225K |

Real companies support this pace. The usual Series A bar is about **$2.5M to $3.3M in revenue** ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/series-a-metrics-vcs-expect)), which the base case reaches in year 3. Infrastructure and security companies grow slower than clinical apps. Protect AI had an estimated **$5M revenue** when Palo Alto bought it ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/protect-ai/financials)), and Skyflow reached "double-digit millions" after about five years and $100M raised ([Yahoo/TechCrunch](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/skyflow-raises-30m-more-ai-180745781.html)). Abridge's jump to about $100M ARR came from deals with the big medical-records companies (EHR vendors) and a product doctors use directly ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/abridge)). An invisible compliance layer will not grow like that. The most likely big win is a sale to a strategic buyer, since **eight major security vendors spent over $2B buying AI-security startups**, many with small revenue ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/securing-ais-ma-feeding-frenzy-is-on/)). The downside case is also real: a missing year-1 customer could mean zero revenue, and cloud guardrails bundled with BAAs could flatten prices.

### The odds of raising a seed

No public data measures how often a given founder raises money, so the numbers below are **my estimates**, not measurements. "Raising a seed" here means $1M or more from institutional investors (funds, not just friends) within 12 months, by November 2027. "Pre-seed" means $250K or more from angels or small funds. Three data points anchor the estimates. Solo founders are about **36% of new startups but only about 17% to 20% of funded ones**, so a solo founder is roughly half as likely as average to raise ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership-2026/); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-state-of-seed-today-10-key-learnings-from-cartas-latest-data)). Solo-led startups got just **14.7% of priced-round cash** ([Carta Solo Founders](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report)). And capital is piling into a few big deals: 20 rounds took 45% of digital health money in H1 2026 ([Rock Health](https://rockhealth.com/insights/h1-2026-funding-and-market-overview/)). In healthcare, investors care most about distribution, because hospital purchasing teams decide what gets bought ([Galen Growth](https://www.galengrowth.com/investor-thesis-health-management-solutions-funding-2026/)). That makes a signed partner the biggest single lever.

| Scenario | Pre-seed ($250K+) | Institutional seed ($1M+) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| As-is: solo, no customers, 83% recall | 10-15% | **3-6%** | Hot category and working code, but the solo penalty, no proof of demand and a recall gap. Most investors will say "come back with customers." |
| + 2-3 design partners or LOIs (at least one a health system or funded health-AI vendor) | 25-35% | **12-18%** | Answers the top question (will anyone buy?). The solo discount and long hospital sales cycles remain. |
| + co-founder (sales or clinical-compliance), no partners yet | 15-20% | **6-10%** | Removes the solo penalty, but demand is still unproven. |
| + co-founder **and** 2-3 partners | 35-45% | **20-30%** | Matches the profile of funded AI-security seeds. Recall at 95%+ pushes it toward the top of the range. |
| Accepted to YC or speedrun | $500K guaranteed | **55-70%** for a further $1.5M+ within 6 months of demo day | The accelerator stamp, investor access and demo day sharply raise conversion. My estimate; no public statistic was found. |
| Chance of *getting into* YC or speedrun | n/a | **1-3% as-is; 4-8% with co-founder + partners** | Unofficial estimate of about 1% to 1.5% for solo technical founders ([YC Insight](https://ycinsight.com/answers/yc-acceptance-rate)); healthcare AI is on YC's wish list. |

The math favors traction first. Applying to YC as-is gives about a 1% to 3% chance of a 60%-plus outcome, which nets out to about 1% to 2%. Spending 90 days to land partners and a co-founder lifts both the direct-raise odds (to 20% to 30%) and the accelerator odds. Even then, most outcomes are "no." A plan B that does not need venture money must exist.

## Recommendation: keep building, but narrow the product and set a 90-day gate

**Keep going, with conditions.** There is a real reason to continue. Documented leaks of PHI to AI, a governance gap (only about 24% of organizations enforce a policy), an open lane between policy tools and redaction gateways, and a buyer pool of strategic acquirers paying $150M to $700M for AI control points all point the same way. But Axon Health should not be pitched as a federated platform of AI subsidiaries. It should be pitched as **"a self-hosted, scored HIPAA gateway for AI agents and LLM apps: deterministic PHI redaction, AI judges that decide, and an audit trail your governance committee can use."** The agent-to-agent design stays as the long-term edge, not the headline. And the next 90 days should test demand, not add features.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Can this redact PHI?" is answered, and the answer has become cheap. Google does it for pennies, and model vendors now sign BAAs. The scarce thing is **trust you can measure**: proof of what slipped through, what was blocked and why, written down per message, on infrastructure the hospital controls. Axon's habit of scoring itself on blind holdouts, and its rule that only fixed code redacts while AI judges only decide, are the real product. No competitor found publishes both recall and over-redaction numbers. That evidence-first stance is what a cautious hospital security team, and an acquirer like Palo Alto or Check Point, would pay for.

The second shift is timing. Federal pressure is easing, so buyers will move because of incidents and accreditation, not deadlines. That favors a founder who can show up right after a leak with a working, self-hosted fix and a published accuracy number. The code is ahead of the business. Closing that gap is a sales problem for the next 90 days, and its result should decide whether Axon Health becomes a company.
